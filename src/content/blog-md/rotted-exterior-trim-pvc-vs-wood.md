You push a screwdriver into the corner board by the back steps and it sinks in. The paint on the window sill has been peeling for two summers. The fascia under one gutter is soft. Rotted trim is a frustrating repair, because a patch can fail again in a few years if the cause is still there.

The fix has two parts: stop the water that caused the rot, and replace the damaged trim with a material that suits that spot. This article covers both, including what to know about cellular PVC trim and when wood still makes sense.

## Why trim rots

Wood rot is caused by decay fungi, and those fungi need water. A chapter on wood's biological properties by the USDA Forest Service's Forest Products Laboratory explains that decay fungi need moisture above the fiber saturation point, about 30% moisture content, to grow, and that free water from rain, condensation or wet ground contact must be present to reach it. The same chapter notes that air-dried wood usually has no more than 20% moisture content, so decay does not occur.

In other words, trim that stays dry does not rot. Trim that gets wet and stays wet does. That is why rot tends to show up first where water collects or dries slowly, such as:

- **Bottom ends of corner boards and casings**, where water collects at the cut ends.
- **Window sills and the bottom of door casings**, where water sits on a flat surface.
- **Fascia and rake boards under gutters** that overflow or leak.
- **Trim close to the ground, steps or a roof**, where splashback and snow keep it wet.
- **Joints with failed caulk or paint**, which let water in and slow drying.

The Forest Products Laboratory chapter also mentions a small group of water-conducting fungi that can carry water into drier wood, so not every case fits the pattern exactly. Most do.

## Step one: find and stop the water

If you replace rotted trim without fixing the water source, the new trim faces the same conditions. The EPA's guide to mold and moisture puts it simply: "The key to mold control is moisture control," and cleaning up without fixing the water problem means the problem will likely return. The same guide recommends cleaning and repairing gutters regularly and making sure the ground slopes away from the foundation.

Before choosing a trim material, look at:

1. **Gutters and downspouts.** Are they overflowing at the corner where the fascia is soft?
2. **Flashing.** Is there a drip cap or head flashing over windows and doors? Is there flashing where a roof meets a wall?
3. **Clearances.** Is trim touching the ground, a deck surface or a roof?
4. **Caulk and paint.** Are joints open?
5. **Hidden damage.** Is the sheathing or framing behind the trim also soft?

If you find mold on the inside of the wall, the EPA guide says cleanup of areas larger than about 10 square feet calls for professional guidance.

## Option one: cellular PVC trim

Cellular PVC trim is used in the same places as wood trim: fascia, rake boards, corner boards, casings and sills. Its main advantage in a wet spot is that it does not absorb water the way wood does. AZEK, one manufacturer, says in its 2024 installation guide that its trim absorbs almost no moisture.

That same property changes how it has to be installed.

### It moves with temperature

AZEK's guide says its trim and moulding expand and contract with changes in temperature. Its instructions to control that:

- Fasten the trim along its entire length into a flat, solid substrate, with fasteners hitting solid framing.
- Use two fasteners per framing member for trimboards, with additional fasteners on wider boards, and fasteners within 2 inches of each end.
- Use fasteners long enough to penetrate solid wood at least 1-1/2 inches, and two fasteners on each side of a joint.
- When properly fastened, allow 1/8 inch per 18 feet for expansion and contraction.
- Glue joints between pieces, and glue the trim to the substrate in addition to fastening it.

### Paint color matters

AZEK's Premium trim does not require paint, according to the guide, but can be painted. If you paint it, AZEK recommends 100% acrylic latex in colors with a Light Reflective Value (LRV) of 55 or higher. For darker colors with an LRV below 55, the paint must come from a vinyl-safe color palette. The guide warns that dark colors may increase movement. It also notes that paint may take longer to cure on PVC than on wood.

If you want dark trim, ask the contractor which paint and which color will be used, and check the LRV before you choose.

### Other manufacturers

Other PVC trim brands publish their own instructions, which may differ. The contractor should be following the instructions for the product actually installed.

## Option two: wood trim

Wood is still a reasonable choice in many places, especially on protected parts of the house, on historic homes where the commission or the owner wants wood, and for custom profiles.

If you stay with wood, the same moisture rules apply. The Forest Products Laboratory chapter recommends that if wood cannot be kept dry, preservative-treated lumber should be used. For trim, that means paying attention to:

- Where the trim sits relative to ground, roofs and decks.
- Keeping end grain sealed and painted, especially at the bottom of vertical boards.
- Keeping paint and caulk maintained so water does not stay in the wood.

## Choosing by location

You do not have to use one material for the whole house. One way to decide is spot by spot:

- **Wet or hard-to-reach spots** like sills, the bottom of corner boards, trim near grade, and fascia under gutters are where a material that does not absorb water helps most.
- **Protected areas and detailed profiles** may stay wood, especially if you want to match original moulding.
- **Dark paint colors** need extra care with PVC because of movement.

## Older houses and lead paint

If your house was built before 1978, the paint on existing trim may contain lead. Under the Massachusetts regulation 454 CMR 22.00, paid exterior work that disturbs more than 20 square feet of painted surface is regulated lead-safe renovation work. Removing a few runs of fascia and corner boards can pass that quickly. Ask your contractor how they will handle it.
