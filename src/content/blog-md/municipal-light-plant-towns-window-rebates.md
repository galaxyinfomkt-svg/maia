Many Massachusetts homeowners plan a window or door project around Mass Save, then find out their town's electricity does not come from a Mass Save company. If you live in a town with its own municipal light plant, the incentives you can use may come from a different program, with different rules and a different application.

This article explains how to tell which situation you are in, what options exist for light plant customers, and what to check before you sign a window contract.

## Who runs Mass Save

Mass Save is a statewide energy efficiency effort run by six sponsors: **Berkshire Gas, Cape Light Compact, Eversource, Liberty, National Grid and Unitil.** None of them is a municipal light plant.

Mass Save's own program pages tie many incentives to being a customer of a sponsor. For example, its business heat pump page limits certain incentives to customers who receive electric service from a sponsor, and it allows gas-side offers for customers who get natural gas from a sponsor, including in municipal electric territories. Residential window rules are set on Mass Save's windows page, and they can change, so read the current version before you count on any rebate.

## Towns with a municipal light plant

We checked the state's MassGIS electric provider map for the Central Massachusetts and MetroWest towns we look at most often. These towns are served by a municipal light plant:

- **Central Massachusetts:** Paxton, Holden, Shrewsbury, West Boylston, Princeton, Boylston and Sterling
- **Hudson and Stow:** Hudson Light and Power Department
- **Boxborough and Littleton:** Littleton Electric Light and Water Department
- **Other towns:** Wellesley, Concord, Norwood, Belmont, Braintree, Reading, Wakefield, Peabody and Danvers

Groton is split, with part of the town served by National Grid. Charlton, by contrast, is served by National Grid.

The quickest way to know for sure is to look at your electric bill. If it comes from the town or a municipal department, you are a light plant customer.

> Check two bills before planning incentives: who supplies your electricity, and who supplies your heating fuel.

## Why your heating fuel matters too

Electric service and heating are separate questions. Some homes in light plant towns heat with natural gas bought from a company that is a Mass Save sponsor. Mass Save's business heat pump page, for example, extends gas-side offers to gas customers of a sponsor in municipal electric territories. If you heat with sponsor gas, ask Mass Save which residential offers, if any, apply to you through your gas account.

If you heat with oil, propane or electricity in a light plant town, the light plant and the state programs below are where to look.

## What light plant customers can look at

### Your light plant's own program

Many light plants run their own efficiency incentives. Two examples:

- **Shrewsbury (SELCO)** publishes a 2026 Home Efficiency Incentive rebate form covering upgrades such as insulation and duct sealing. The form says self-installed insulation is not eligible, and renters should get the owner's approval before installing any measures.
- **Hudson Light and Power** describes conservation rebate programs for residential and commercial customers and offers help with questions about measures such as insulation.

We did not find window-specific rebates listed in the materials we reviewed for these utilities. Programs change from year to year, so call your light plant and ask directly whether windows or doors qualify, and what paperwork is needed.

### The state's MLP zero-interest loan

The Massachusetts Department of Energy Resources runs the **MLP Z-Loan**, a zero-interest loan for residential homeowners served by municipal light plants who are not eligible for the Mass Save HEAT Loan. The state page says the loans cover efficiency and electrification upgrades including heat pumps, heat pump water heaters, insulation improvements and weatherization barriers. Applications go through [myz-loan.com](https://www.myz-loan.com).

Whether windows qualify is not stated in the summary we reviewed. Ask the program before you assume it covers a window project. Some light plants also note limits: Shrewsbury's page, for example, says Eversource gas customers are not eligible for the DOER Z-Loan but can use SELCO's own loan program.

## Three quick examples

- **Charlton, National Grid electricity.** Charlton is served by a Mass Save sponsor, so the Mass Save windows page is the place to start.
- **Shrewsbury, SELCO electricity and Eversource gas heat.** SELCO's page says Eversource gas customers are not eligible for the DOER Z-Loan but can use SELCO's own loan program. Mass Save may also have gas-side offers through the Eversource gas account. Check both before ordering.
- **Holden, oil heat.** Holden is served by a municipal light plant, and the Mass Save pages we reviewed tie electric-side incentives to sponsor customers. Start with the light plant's program and the DOER MLP Z-Loan.

## What to check before you sign a window contract

1. **Your electric supplier.** Light plant or Mass Save sponsor?
2. **Your gas supplier, if any.** Is it a Mass Save sponsor?
3. **The program's current rules.** Which windows qualify, which performance ratings are required, and whether a pre-approval or a specific contractor list applies.
4. **Timing.** Some programs require you to apply before work starts. Find out before you place an order.
5. **Paperwork.** Ask what documents the program needs from the contractor: invoice, product labels or ratings, photos.
6. **Federal credits.** The federal window and door tax credit ended for property placed in service after December 31, 2025. See [what ended and what is left in 2026](/blog/window-door-tax-credit-2026-status).
