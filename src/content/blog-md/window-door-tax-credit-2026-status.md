For a few years, homeowners replacing windows and exterior doors could count on a federal tax credit to take some of the sting out of the bill. A lot of quotes, ads and articles still mention it. If you are planning a project now, here is the straight answer: the federal Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit, known as Section 25C, does not apply to windows or doors placed in service in 2026.

This article explains what ended, what that means if your project straddled the deadline, and what to check instead.

## What the credit was

The IRS describes the Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit as 30% of certain qualified expenses for property placed in service from January 1, 2023, through December 31, 2025. For the categories that matter to window and door projects, the IRS listed these limits:

- **Windows and skylights:** credit limited to $600 total, for products that meet ENERGY STAR Most Efficient certification requirements.
- **Exterior doors:** credit limited to $250 per door and $500 total.
- **Annual cap:** $1,200 per year for energy efficient property costs and certain home improvements, with a separate $2,000 yearly cap for heat pumps, water heaters and biomass stoves or boilers.

ENERGY STAR added that the windows and skylights credit was available only for a home owned and used by the taxpayer as a principal residence.

## What ended it

Public Law 119-21, signed July 4, 2025 and commonly called the One, Big, Beautiful Bill, moved up the end of the credit. The IRS FAQ on the law says the 25C credit **will not be allowed for any property placed in service after December 31, 2025.**

> For windows and doors, the federal 25C credit is not available for property placed in service in 2026 or later.

### "Placed in service" is the test

The IRS ties the end of the credit to when the property is placed in service, and that date can be later than when you signed the contract or paid a deposit. If you ordered windows or doors in late 2025 but they were installed in 2026, talk to a tax professional before assuming you can claim anything.

## If your project was finished in 2025

If your qualifying windows or doors were installed in 2025, the credit is claimed on your 2025 federal return, which most people file in 2026. The IRS says to file **Form 5695**, Residential Energy Credits, Part II, with your return.

For property placed in service in 2025, there is an added requirement. The IRS says you must report the **Qualified Manufacturer Identification Number (QMID)** for each item on your return. The 2025 Form 5695 instructions describe it as a four-character alphanumeric code. Ask your contractor or the manufacturer for it, along with your invoice and the product's ENERGY STAR or NFRC information.

Keep those records with your tax files.

### Common mix-ups on 2025 claims

- **Any ENERGY STAR window.** For windows and skylights, the IRS required products that meet ENERGY STAR Most Efficient certification requirements, which is a narrower group than ENERGY STAR certified windows in general. ENERGY STAR tells shoppers to check a product's CPD number in the NFRC database for their climate zone.
- **Second homes and rentals.** ENERGY STAR says the windows and skylights credit was available only for a home owned and used by the taxpayer as a principal residence.
- **Doors and windows sharing a cap.** Doors had their own $250 per door and $500 total limits, and both counted toward the $1,200 annual cap.

## What is still worth checking in 2026

With the federal credit gone, the incentives that remain for windows and doors are mostly state and utility programs. They come with their own rules:

- **Mass Save.** If your electricity or gas comes from a Mass Save sponsor, check the current Mass Save windows page for what is offered, which products qualify and what documentation is required. We do not quote amounts here because they change.
- **Municipal light plants.** If your town runs its own electric utility, your incentives may come from the light plant instead of Mass Save. See [municipal light plant towns and window rebates](/blog/municipal-light-plant-towns-window-rebates).
- **State loans.** The Department of Energy Resources runs a zero-interest MLP Z-Loan for some light plant customers. Check what it covers before planning around it.

### Questions to ask before you count on any incentive

1. Am I eligible as a customer of this utility or program?
2. Does the product I am considering meet the program's performance requirements?
3. Do I need approval before the work starts?
4. What paperwork does the contractor need to provide?

## Choosing windows and doors without the credit

The end of the credit does not change what a good window or door does for a house. When you compare products, look at the NFRC label for U-factor, which measures how well the product keeps heat from escaping, and SHGC. Check what your town's energy code requires for replacement units, which is covered in our guide to the [Specialized and Stretch energy codes and window replacement](/blog/specialized-stretch-energy-code-window-replacement).

If someone tells you a 2026 installation qualifies for the federal 25C credit, ask them to show you the IRS guidance that says so.
