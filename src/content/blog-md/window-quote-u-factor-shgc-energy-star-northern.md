Two window quotes can look alike until you notice one lists a U-factor of 0.27 and the other 0.22, one says "ENERGY STAR" and the other says "Low-E," and neither explains what those mean. Window performance is rated in a standard way, so you can compare quotes line by line once you know what to look for.

This article covers the ratings on the NFRC label, what ENERGY STAR requires in the Northern zone (which covers Massachusetts), and how to check a product's numbers yourself.

## The NFRC label: five numbers

The National Fenestration Rating Council (NFRC) rates and labels windows, doors and skylights. Its consumer page describes the ratings on the label:

- **U-Factor:** how well the product keeps heat from escaping a room. Lower is better. NFRC's window label scale runs from 0.20 to 1.20.
- **Solar Heat Gain Coefficient (SHGC):** how much solar heat the product lets in, on a scale of 0 to 1. Low values block solar heat; higher values let more in.
- **Visible Transmittance (VT):** how much daylight comes through, from 0 to 1. Higher means more light.
- **Air Leakage:** how much air will enter a room through the product. NFRC lists the range for this rating as 0.3 or less.
- **Condensation Resistance:** how well the product resists condensation. Higher is better.

The ENERGY STAR specification defines U-factor as heat transfer through the whole fenestration product, measured in Btu per hour per square foot per degree Fahrenheit, so it covers frame and glass together. A quote that only describes the glass ("triple-pane Low-E argon") does not tell you the window's U-factor.

NFRC's FAQ also tells shoppers to look for the NFRC temporary and permanent labels on the product, and states that a product without the sticker is not considered a certified window.

## What ENERGY STAR requires in Massachusetts

ENERGY STAR divides the country into four climate zones for windows. Its climate zone spreadsheet lists every Massachusetts ZIP code in the **Northern** zone. You can confirm your own with the ENERGY STAR Climate Zone Finder.

Under the ENERGY STAR Version 7.0 specification, effective October 23, 2023, a window qualifies in the Northern zone by meeting either path:

- **Prescriptive path:** U-factor of 0.22 or lower and SHGC of 0.17 or higher.
- **Equivalent energy performance path:** a slightly higher U-factor paired with a higher minimum SHGC, set out in a separate table in the specification. Equivalent performance is only offered for windows in the Northern zone.

The specification also requires an air leakage rating of 0.3 cfm per square foot or less for windows.

> In the Northern zone, ENERGY STAR sets a minimum SHGC. Letting in some winter sun counts in your favor here.

### Why there is a minimum SHGC

In the other three zones, ENERGY STAR caps SHGC to limit solar heat gain. In the Northern zone it does the opposite for windows and sets a floor, so a window whose glass blocks too much solar heat will not qualify on the prescriptive path. If a quote shows a very low SHGC, ask why that glass package was chosen.

### Doors and skylights have their own limits

The same specification rates swinging doors by how much glass they have. Opaque doors need a U-factor of 0.17 or lower, doors with half-lite glass or less need 0.23 or lower, and doors with more than half-lite glass need 0.26 or lower in the Northern and North-Central zones. Skylights in the Northern zone need a U-factor of 0.45 or lower. If your project includes a door or skylight, check those lines separately.

## Reading a quote line by line

When you have two or more quotes, put these side by side for each window type:

1. **Product line and model.** Is it the same window, or a different series from the same brand?
2. **U-factor and SHGC taken from the NFRC rating**, rather than a description of the glass.
3. **ENERGY STAR status** for the Northern zone, and which path it meets.
4. **Air leakage rating.**
5. **Options and the rating.** Ask whether choices such as grilles or a different glass package change the certified numbers, and make sure the rating on the quote matches the exact configuration you are buying.
6. **Installation scope.** Is it an insert into the existing frame or a full-frame replacement? What happens with interior and exterior trim, flashing and insulation around the unit?
7. **Disposal and cleanup.**

A difference of a few hundredths in U-factor between two quotes is worth less than a difference in what the installation includes. A well-rated window installed poorly can still leak air and water around the frame.

## How to check the numbers yourself

You do not have to take the quote's word for it.

- **NFRC Certified Products Directory.** NFRC's directory has a label verification search where you enter the U-factor, SHGC and VT from a label to confirm it matches a certified product. You can also search by product type or by CPD number.
- **ENERGY STAR product finder.** Look up the model to confirm it is certified for the Northern zone.
- **Ask for the label or spec sheet** before you sign, and check that the numbers match the configuration on the quote.

## A few related points

- **Older homes:** In a house built before 1978, Massachusetts treats window replacement as regulated lead-safe renovation work regardless of size under 454 CMR 22.00. Ask how the contractor will handle it.
- **Rebates:** Utility programs have their own product requirements. Check the current program rules before you choose a product, and keep the NFRC label and invoice.
- **Comfort:** Lower U-factors mainly reduce heat loss through the window. Drafts around the frame are an installation and air sealing question, so ask about both.
