Once you decide the siding has to go, the next question is when. Spring feels natural, but so does fall, and some homeowners wonder whether a winter job is a bad idea or just a cold one. The answer depends on the material, the weather the crew will work in, and what has to happen after the siding goes up, such as painting.

This article uses 30 years of temperature data for Central Massachusetts and the published installation instructions for vinyl and fiber cement siding to show what each season means for the job.

## What the weather looks like, month by month

Here are the 1991 to 2020 normal daily high and low temperatures at Worcester Regional Airport, from the NOAA Regional Climate Centers' ACIS data service. Your town may run a few degrees warmer or cooler depending on elevation.

- **January:** high 32°F, low 17°F
- **February:** high 35°F, low 19°F
- **March:** high 43°F, low 26°F
- **April:** high 56°F, low 37°F
- **May:** high 67°F, low 47°F
- **June:** high 75°F, low 56°F
- **July:** high 80°F, low 62°F
- **August:** high 78°F, low 61°F
- **September:** high 71°F, low 53°F
- **October:** high 59°F, low 42°F
- **November:** high 48°F, low 33°F
- **December:** high 38°F, low 23°F

From May through October, normal lows stay above freezing and normal highs are well above 50°F. From December through March, normal highs are near or below the low 40s.

## Vinyl siding and cold weather

Vinyl siding can be installed in cold weather, but the crew has to adjust for it.

The installation guidance published by the Polymeric Exterior Products Association (the industry group behind the VSI installation manual) says vinyl siding can expand and contract 1/2 inch or more over a 12-foot-6-inch length as temperatures change. Its tips for installers say:

- Leave a minimum of 1/4 inch clearance at all openings and stops.
- **When installing in temperatures below 40°F, increase that minimum clearance to 3/8 inch.**
- Do not drive nail heads tight against the nailing hem; leave about 1/32 inch of clearance so the panel can move.
- When cutting with a circular saw, cut slowly, especially in cold weather.

So a vinyl job in November or March is possible, but panels put on in the cold have to be spaced and nailed for the summer expansion that will come later. On a job that runs from a warm week into a cold one, the gaps should change with the conditions. Ask your contractor how they handle that.

## Fiber cement and wet weather

For fiber cement, moisture and paint scheduling matter more than air temperature during installation.

James Hardie's HardiePlank lap siding installation requirements (December 2019 edition) say:

- **Store the siding flat, dry and covered** before installation. Installing siding wet or saturated may result in shrinkage at butt joints.
- **Factory-primed product must be painted within 180 days** of installation.
- **Do not paint when wet**, and follow the paint manufacturer's written instructions on application temperature.
- **Apply caulk according to the caulk manufacturer's written instructions.**

The 180-day window gives some flexibility. Primed siding installed late in the fall can be painted when spring brings temperatures the paint label allows, as long as that falls within 180 days. Factory-finished products do not need field painting, though touch-up and caulking still follow their own label limits.

## What each season means for your project

### Spring (April to June)

Temperatures rise into ranges most paint and caulk labels allow, and days are getting longer. Spring also brings rain, so schedule around wet days, especially for fiber cement that should not be installed or painted wet. Getting an estimate during the winter leaves time for measuring, ordering and any permits before a spring start.

### Summer (July to August)

The warmest months. For vinyl, the guidance on gaps still applies; panels installed on a hot day are already expanded, so nailing and spacing still need to follow the manual. Some paint and caulk labels also list an upper temperature limit, so check them on hot days.

### Fall (September to October)

Normal highs in the 60s and 70s in September and the high 50s in October make fall a comfortable season for exterior work. Normal lows stay above freezing through October. If you want the job finished before snow, plan the start date so the work, including any painting, wraps up before late fall.

### Winter (November to March)

Possible for some jobs, with care. Vinyl needs the cold-weather clearances. Fiber cement has to stay dry, and painting may wait until spring. Snow on the ground, short days and frozen grade around the foundation can slow the work. If a wall is damaged and letting water in, a winter repair is usually better than waiting for spring.

## Plan around more than the weather

A few other things affect timing:

- **Permits.** Your town's building department may need to issue a permit before work starts. Ask early.
- **Material lead times.** Some colors, profiles and trim pieces have to be ordered.
- **Windows and doors.** If you are replacing windows or doors in the same project, sequence them with the siding so the flashing around each opening laps correctly.
- **Lead-safe work on older homes.** If your house was built before 1978, removing old siding and trim can disturb painted surfaces regulated under 454 CMR 22.00. Ask how the contractor will contain and clean up the work.
- **Historic districts.** In a local historic district, commission review under Chapter 40C may need to happen first.
